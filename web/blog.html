Six things worth writing down

Each card summarises a document or a test in the repository and links straight to it.

Security · 7 min

Why a safety number beats a trust-on-first-use prompt

Pairing does an authenticated ECDH and then asks two humans to read six words to each other. That second step is the whole point: it moves the trust decision off the network the attacker controls.

Read the threat model ›

Networking · 6 min

Surviving a Wi-Fi to cellular switch without a black screen

A default-network callback arms an ICE restart on the same peer connection. The video track and data channel survive, so the person being helped is never asked to consent twice — the design is built and unit-tested, and still waiting on a real network switch to confirm it.

How reconnection works ›

Android · 8 min

The honest ceiling on unattended Android access

Android 11 made a MediaProjection grant effectively single-use and Android 14 tightened re-acquisition. Here is what that means for real unattended support, and the design it forces.

Read the constraint ›

Windows · 9 min

Desktop Duplication to VP8, and why two SIPSorcery peers prove less than you think

The full chain is implemented and passes between in-process peers through the real broker. Both ends being the same library means they could share a misreading of VP8 packetisation — so it stays a preview until a phone renders it.

Read the pipeline notes ›

Infrastructure · 5 min

Why the broker keeps everything in memory

Registrations, codes and pending wakes are all ephemeral by design. Restarting the broker cannot leak history that was never written down, and live sessions simply re-establish.

Running the broker ›

Protocol · 6 min

Cross-language fixtures, so two implementations cannot drift

The same pinned fixture files are read by the Kotlin, C# and Node suites — including the safety number two people read aloud, which all three compute identically. A protocol change that only one side understands fails CI before it reaches a device.

Read the protocol spec ›
